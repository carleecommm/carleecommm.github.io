Craft

Four disciplines, one aim: simpler systems

  • 01

    Service design

    Mapping how a service works from end to end, for customers and for the staff who run it, to find where it lets people down and what to fix first.

  • 02

    UX research

    Interviews, surveys, observation, and usability testing, written up and then built into a system: repositories and guides that keep findings findable, so teams reuse what was learned rather than running the research again.

  • 03

    Content design

    Help content, interface copy, style guides, and content models. More recently, AI-assisted workflows that help content teams work faster, and the system prompts behind them.

  • 04

    Interaction design

    Working out the path a user needs to take through a complex screen, then specifying it: flows, states, keyboard navigation, and accessibility, written so developers can build from it.

Campaign Monitor · 2016–17

Redesigning self-service support

Service designContent designUX research

Customers were getting help instructions for the wrong type of account. I led the redesign of the help centre so one set of content could serve three customer audiences.

Help articles

430 → 270

and 18% fewer support cases in repeat-question categories

Read the Help Scout case study

The problem

As Campaign Monitor added new customer types and account models, its help content didn't keep up. Customers were shown instructions for the wrong account type, and support agents had to work out which version applied.

What I did

Nobody asked for this project. I spent a year making the case for it — slide decks, presentations, whiteboard sketches for executives who weren't technical — until I had sponsorship and budget. I shadowed support agents and answered customer tickets myself to see where the effort went. Engineering estimated 1.5 developers for 12 weeks and that was refused, so I found an external agency and negotiated what could be built on a small budget: 4.5 weeks of their Salesforce developers, co-designed with a teammate from their office.

The result

430 articles became 270, serving three customer audiences from one content base. Support cases in repeat-question categories fell by 18%.

Atlassian · 2024–26

AI workflows for support documentation

Content designService designAI workflows

I designed ways for a new documentation team to use AI to draft support content, and improved an AI pipeline that suggested changes to that content.

Acceptance of AI-suggested changes

+7.86%

in two months

The problem

A newly formed central documentation team needed to produce support content faster. At the same time, a company programme to reduce support costs relied on an AI pipeline that used support tickets to suggest documentation changes.

What I did

I designed intake forms and a repeatable AI-assisted pattern for drafting briefs, which turned scattered project materials into clear inputs for content designers. As documentation lead on the cost programme, I coordinated content designers to build the reference data set used to train the model, and I rewrote the pipeline's system prompt.

The result

Acceptance of the pipeline's suggested changes rose 7.86% in two months. The team building Atlassian's agentic content workflow adopted the brief-drafting pattern after I showed them how it worked.

LiSTNR, Southern Cross Austereo · 2021

Research the whole team could use

UX researchContent designResearch operations

I established research operations for LiSTNR, and built the repository its design team used to find and reuse findings.

Survey responses

1,600+

from 14,600 subscribers, an 11% response rate

The problem

Southern Cross Austereo was moving listeners from its radio station apps to one app, LiSTNR. The plan rested on assumptions about what listeners wanted, and those assumptions had not been tested.

What I did

I designed and ran a survey of 14,600 Hit Network and Triple M subscribers to test those assumptions, and ran research from discovery through to usability testing. I built the research repository in Dovetail, with user guides and a live feed of app store reviews grouped into themes.

The result

The survey settled assumptions the plan had been resting on, and the findings went into the repository alongside the rest of the team's research, where people could find and reuse them.

Atlassian, Jira Service Desk · 2020

Approval decisions, captured where they happen

Interaction designContent designUX research

Approvers had no way to record why they approved or declined a change request. I designed that step into all three places an approval can happen.

Approval surfaces

3

all covered by one design

The problem

Jira Service Desk showed no screen when a change request moved through an approval step, so approvers had nowhere to record why they approved or declined it. Customers running change management needed that reason captured at the moment of the decision, for audit and for the approval steps that followed.

What I did

Using survey responses from customers running change management in Jira Service Desk, I designed the comment step into each of the three places an approval happens: the agent view, the customer portal, and email notifications. The developer specs covered the user story, the interface and the wording for each surface, and I wrote the explanation of how workflow transitions and screens work for the people who had to build it.

The result

One pattern covered all three surfaces, including approvers who are not service desk agents and never open the agent view.
